India: sector share of GVA

2024–25, approximate shares: services 55%, secondary 29%, primary 16%. GVA is output composition, not employment composition. Source: NITI Aayog, India’s Services Sector.

India: selected employment shares

Agriculture ~46% and manufacturing ~11% in 2023–24; modern market services ~7% in the World Bank report. Modern market services is a narrow category, not all services. Sources: Economic Survey 2025–26 and World Bank report. Period and classification caveats apply.

India: share of global exports

India’s share of global exports, 2005 vs 2024: merchandise 1.0% → 1.8%; commercial services 2.0% → 4.3%. Shares are separate categories, not job shares. Source: Economic Survey 2025–26, Chapter 4.

India and Vietnam: backward GVC participation

Foreign value added as a share of gross exports: India 10.7% (1995), 25.5% (2012), 17.2% (2020); Vietnam about 48% (2020). Different points are shown only where source data is available. Source: Economic Survey 2025–26, Chapter 8 / OECD TiVA.

India: total trade in FY2024–25

Goods + services: exports US$825.3bn, imports US$919.9bn; overall trade deficit US$94.7bn. Services surplus cushioned the merchandise deficit. A deficit is not a direct measure of job losses. Source: Economic Survey 2025–26.

Jobs embodied in exports

Average share across 61 economies: about 23.3% in 1995 and 28.2% in 2019. Includes upstream domestic supplier jobs, not only direct exporter employees. Source: WTO World Trade Report 2024.

Productivity: what changes output?

Conceptual framework, not measured percentage contributions. Factors interact; no universal weights are implied.

Evidence snapshot: India textile experiment

11%

Average productivity increase reported in treatment plants in a management-practices field experiment.

28 plants across 17 large textile firms; study-specific result, not an economy-wide estimate. Bloom et al., World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 5573 (2011).

Stage 2: Why firms exist

Market prices & contracts↔Compare total coordination costs↔Internal hierarchy

Firms internalise work when organising it inside can cost less or coordinate it better than repeated market exchange. Internal management has costs too.

Williamson transaction dimensions: uncertainty · frequency · asset specificity. Parts 1–2 researched; Stage 2 remains in progress.

Stage 2: Make, buy or ally?

MAKE: internal team↔BUY: supplier↔ALLY: long-term partner

Compare lifecycle cost, not just salary or supplier quotation.

Check asset specificity · uncertainty · frequency · measurability · switching cost · innovation and control.

Evidence snapshot: 2006 meta-analysis: 200 papers, 209 independent samples, N = 91,006. Broad support for make/buy/ally logic; asset specificity was not shown to predict better than uncertainty.

Growth vs development

Growth: expansion in real production, commonly measured with real GDP or GVA.

Development: broader improvements in health, education, opportunity, security, distribution and sustainability.

GDP growth is important, but it is not a complete measure of well-being.

Structural transformation

Agriculture↔Industry↔Services

Workers and capital move between sectors, firms and regions. Aggregate productivity can change through within-sector improvement and reallocation.

Not a universal one-way sequence; services and manufacturing contain both high- and low-productivity activities.
